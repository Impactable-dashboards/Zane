04 / THE OPEN MIDDLE

Four lanes ZANE can own

Each lane is a position the field leaves open, cross-referenced against the buyers the targeting work identified. Rated by how many of those buyers it would move. These are grounded in what ZANE already does, not aspiration.

Lane 1 · Broadcast craft, direct to the brandHigh opportunity

The prestige houses sit behind an ad agency. ZANE does not.

Why it matters: Caviar produces through agencies like Saatchi, and division7 and Huffman represent directors for agency briefs. Their craft comes with a representation layer, a markup, and an agency timeline. ZANE brings comparable craft, from Marvel and Amazon to UFC and major-label work, directly to the brand, founder-led.

Evidence: in-house brand teams increasingly commission production directly to skip the agency markup and the wait. Agency-grade craft without the agency is a lane the prestige tier structurally cannot occupy. This is the lane for the in-house brand leader.
Lane 2 · One shoot, a full campaign libraryHigh opportunity

The prestige tier delivers one hero. The volume tier delivers many generic assets. Neither delivers craft at campaign scale.

Why it matters: modern brands need horizontal, vertical, square, cutdowns, and alternate hooks for every channel, all on brand, from a single production. ZANE's model is built for exactly this.

Evidence: a 50-asset Tillys campaign and a 21-deliverable Valyou shoot, reported at 550% return, show broadcast craft delivered as a campaign system rather than a single film. The specialists do volume or craft, rarely both. This lane serves both the brand leader and the growth buyer.
Lane 3 · Premium craft built to performHigh opportunity

The roster shops optimize for the award and the hero film. Performance creative is an afterthought.

Why it matters: ZANE already produces the paid-social variations, alternate hooks, vertical edits, and platform-native cutdowns that growth teams actually run, at premium quality. That speaks to the demand-generation buyer the prestige tier does not.

Evidence: ZANE's own commercial offering lists paid-ad variations, A/B hooks, and platform-specific edits. This is craft built to be tested and run. Running and measuring that media is a separate job, and the open frontier this room turns to next.
Lane 4 · One partner across every format and categoryMed-High

The market is fragmenting into narrow specialists. ZANE spans the whole slate.

Why it matters: Sandwich owns tech product, Vidico owns SaaS explainer, the medical-animation shops own regulated science, and the prestige houses own entertainment craft. A brand that wants one trusted partner for commercials, social, corporate, music, live, and post, across consumer, tech, and healthcare-brand work, otherwise has to stitch several specialists together.

Evidence: ZANE's range, from a Marvel spot to a Novo Nordisk healthcare commercial to forty social cutdowns, is unusual in one shop. Breadth at broadcast quality is a defensible position as the field narrows into specialists.
05 / THE ONE FRONTIER

Made. Delivered. Then what?

One thing sits beyond all four lanes, and it is the one thing none of these ten do, ZANE included. 09 / THE SHORT VERSION

Key takeaways

  • The market is a barbell: agency-gated prestige craft on one end, fast-but-generic volume on the other. The middle is thin.
  • ZANE's ownable lane is that middle: broadcast craft, delivered directly to the brand, as a full campaign, in weeks, across every format and category.
  • The prestige tier cannot follow ZANE into direct-to-brand and campaign-scale without unwinding the agency-gated, single-hero model that defines it.
  • The volume tier cannot follow ZANE up in craft without rebuilding as a premium shop. The two ends are structurally stuck.
  • The near-term pressure is real: the productized players (Lemonlight, Vidico) have claimed transparent pricing and always-on in the value lane, so the message has to earn the premium.
  • The one frontier none of them hold, ZANE included, is reach and proof: getting the work in front of the right buyers and showing what it produced. That is the growth direction, and it needs a system no video shop runs alone.
  • The single move: stop leading with "award-winning full-service," which every competitor says, and lead with the middle ZANE actually owns, then grow into the frontier the market ignores.

03 / MEASUREMENT

Proof that answers to a CFO

This is the honest answer to the gap the whole market leaves open. Perfect attribution does not exist in B2B, so the discipline is to name the lowest claim the evidence supports and never overclaim. Three rungs, three claims.

Rung 1 · Attribution

Click-verified

Named companies and contacts with tracked click or conversion paths to pipeline. The number that survives CFO scrutiny. Lead here, always.

Rung 2 · Influence

Impression-verified

Named accounts whose exposure timeline overlaps deal creation, shown as influence with the timeline, not claimed as a click.

Rung 3 · Exposure

Delivery, stated plainly

Which accounts received impressions. A fact about delivery, never dressed up as a result.

The first step for ZANE is conversion capture: the Insight Tag and defined events, verified in week one, because reporting is only as good as the capture underneath it. Once a CRM is connected, deal data matches to the accounts that saw or engaged with the ads, so influenced pipeline, closed-won, and closed-lost sit in one view. Here is what that looks like in practice.

What we would not promise

A specific cost per lead before the tracking is verified. Full econometric marketing-mix modeling, which is a specialist discipline we will not claim. And vanity reach as a success metric: the program is measured on qualified pipeline influence, because a program judged on the wrong number can look like failure while it is working.
